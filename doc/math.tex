\section{What we want to be true}

Take $N$ songs, each a vector of audio samples $x_1,\dots,x_N \in \R^D$
(here $N=6$, $D \approx 5.5\times 10^5$ at 1\,kHz). We want a single matrix
$A \in \R^{D\times D}$ that \emph{is} the playlist:
\begin{equation}
  A x_n = x_{n+1} \quad (n = 1,\dots,N-1), \qquad A x_N = x_1.
  \label{eq:playlist}
\end{equation}
Feed it any song of the playlist and one matrix--vector product returns the
next song, forever, cyclically. Stacking the songs as columns of
$\X = [\,x_1 \cdots x_N\,] \in \R^{D\times N}$ and writing
$\Xn = [\,x_2, \dots, x_N, x_1\,]$ (columns advanced one step), all $N$
conditions in \eqref{eq:playlist} become one matrix equation:
\begin{equation}
  A\X = \Xn .
  \label{eq:AX}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The key count.} Equation \eqref{eq:AX} imposes $DN$ scalar
constraints on the $D^2$ entries of $A$. Since $N \ll D$, the solution set is
a huge affine family: roughly $D^2 - DN \approx D(D-N)$ dimensions of freedom
remain. \emph{The songs are low rank} --- they span only an $N$-dimensional
subspace $\mathcal{S} = \operatorname{span}(x_1,\dots,x_N) \subset \R^D$ ---
so \eqref{eq:AX} pins down how $A$ acts on $\mathcal{S}$ and says
\emph{nothing} about how it acts on the orthogonal complement
$\mathcal{S}^\perp$. That freedom is where the artwork lives.

\section{Solving \texorpdfstring{$A\X = \Xn$}{AX = Xnext}, and why \texorpdfstring{$G = \X^\top\X$}{G = XtX} appears}

\paragraph{Coefficients in the song basis.} Given any vector $v \in \R^D$,
its best approximation inside $\mathcal S$ is a combination $\X c$ with
coefficients $c \in \R^N$ chosen by least squares:
\begin{equation}
  \min_c \norm{\X c - v}^2
  \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
  \X^\top \X\, c = \X^\top v
  \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
  c = G^{-1}\X^\top v, \qquad G \equiv \X^\top\X \in \R^{N\times N}.
  \label{eq:coeffs}
\end{equation}
$G$ is the \emph{Gram matrix} of the songs: $G_{ij} = x_i^\top x_j$ collects
all pairwise song correlations. It is invertible exactly when the songs are
linearly independent (for real recordings they always are; our measured
condition number is $1.01$, i.e.\ the songs are nearly orthogonal). The
operator
\begin{equation}
  P \;=\; \X G^{-1}\X^\top \qquad (\text{size } D \times D,\ \text{rank } N)
\end{equation}
is the \emph{orthogonal projector onto} $\mathcal S$: it reads a vector,
computes its song coefficients \eqref{eq:coeffs}, and reassembles the part of
the vector that lies in song space. Its complement
\begin{equation}
  \Pperp \;=\; I - \X G^{-1}\X^\top
\end{equation}
keeps only what is orthogonal to every song; in particular
$\Pperp x_n = 0$ for all $n$.

\paragraph{The minimum-norm solution.} Now solve \eqref{eq:AX}. Try
$A_0 = \Xn\, G^{-1} \X^\top$ and check it:
\begin{equation}
  A_0 \X
  = \Xn\, \underbrace{G^{-1}\X^\top \X}_{=\;G^{-1}G\;=\;I_N}
  = \Xn. \qquad \checkmark
  \label{eq:A0check}
\end{equation}
This is why $G^{-1}$ is the essential quantity: it \emph{unmixes} the song
correlations so that each $x_n$ is mapped to exactly its own successor and
nothing else. Read $A_0 = \Xn (G^{-1}\X^\top)$ right to left: correlate the
input against all songs ($\X^\top$), unmix ($G^{-1}$), emit the
corresponding successors ($\Xn$). $A_0$ is the \emph{smallest} solution (minimum
Frobenius norm), and it annihilates everything orthogonal to the songs.

\paragraph{The full solution family.} If $A$ and $A'$ both satisfy
\eqref{eq:AX}, their difference satisfies $(A - A')\X = 0$, i.e.\ it kills
song space --- which means it can be written as (anything)$\,\times \Pperp$.
Hence \emph{every} exact playlist operator has the form
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;A \;=\; A_0 + Z\,\Pperp, \qquad Z \in \R^{D\times D}
  \text{ completely free}.\;}
  \label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
The free part is invisible to playback: $Z \Pperp x_n = Z\,0 = 0$. Playback
exactness is enforced \emph{by construction}, not by optimization; our code
verifies $\max_n \norm{A x_n - x_{n+1}}/\norm{x_{n+1}} \approx 10^{-14}$
(floating-point roundoff) for every candidate artwork.

\section{Rendering: how a \texorpdfstring{$D\times D$}{DxD} matrix becomes a \texorpdfstring{$P\times P$}{PxP} picture}

$A$ has $D^2 \approx 3\times10^{11}$ entries --- never materialized. It is kept
in factored form $A = LR^\top$ with
$L = [\,\Xn G^{-1} \mid U\,]$, $R = [\,\X \mid \Pperp V\,]$ when
$Z = UV^\top$ is low rank. A $P\times P$ picture must summarize
$k \times k$ blocks of matrix entries, $k = \lfloor D/P \rfloor$. Define the
block-pooling operator $\pool \in \R^{P\times D}$ (row $p$ averages samples in
block $p$). Two natural per-block statistics:
\begin{align}
  \text{block mean:}\quad
  M &= \pool A \pool^\top = (\pool L)(\pool R)^\top
  && \text{(linear in $A$)} \label{eq:mean}\\
  \text{block energy:}\quad
  E_{pq} &= \tfrac1{k^2}\textstyle\sum_{i\in p,\, j\in q} A_{ij}^2
  = \operatorname{tr}(G_p H_q)
  && \text{(quadratic in $A$)} \label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
with small per-block Gram matrices
$G_p = \frac1k\sum_{i \in p} L_i^{\vphantom\top} L_i^\top$,
$H_q = \frac1k\sum_{j \in q} R_j^{\vphantom\top} R_j^\top$
($L_i$ = $i$-th row of $L$). Both stream through the factors; cost scales
with pixels, never $D^2$.

\emph{Why two channels?} The matrix is one object; these are two different
summaries of the same $A$, and the choice is purely presentational. They
show different things: audio oscillates around zero, so inside any block the
entries of $A_0$ cancel and its \emph{mean} picture is nearly blank --- but
their \emph{squares} do not cancel, so the energy picture reveals the
song-block structure (loudness correlations, silence bands). Conversely, the
smooth free part $Z$ shows up strongly in the mean channel. For the embedded
artwork below we display the mean channel: it is the channel that is linear
in $A$, hence fully controllable.

\section{Making the picture be an image of your choice}

Here is the reframing. Because \eqref{eq:mean} is \emph{linear} in $A$ and
the free part of $A$ is enormous, the picture is almost unconstrained. Put
$Z = UV^\top$ (rank $q$) into \eqref{eq:mean}:
\begin{equation}
  M \;=\; \underbrace{\pool A_0 \pool^\top}_{M_0\ \text{(fixed by the music)}}
  \;+\; (\pool U)\,\bigl(\pool\, \Pperp V\bigr)^{\!\top}.
  \label{eq:Mdecomp}
\end{equation}
A $P \times P$ target image $T$ has $P^2$ numbers ($\approx 10^5$); $Z$
offers $\approx D(D-N) \approx 3\times10^{11}$ free parameters. The counting
is overwhelmingly favorable --- the constraint is not the image, it is only
that a rank-$N$ sliver ($\Pperp$) is reserved for the music. Concretely, we
want to choose $U, V$ such that
\begin{equation}
  (\pool U)(\pool \Pperp V)^\top \;=\; T_{\mathrm{amp}} - M_0
  \;\equiv\; \Delta,
  \label{eq:embedgoal}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{amp}}$ is the target after centering and amplitude scaling
(display is affine-invariant, so this loses nothing).

\paragraph{Step 1: factor the image (SVD).} The truncated singular value
decomposition
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \;\approx\; W_q \Sigma_q Y_q^\top
  = \bigl(W_q \Sigma_q^{1/2}\bigr)\bigl(Y_q \Sigma_q^{1/2}\bigr)^{\!\top}
  \;\equiv\; \widetilde U\, \widetilde V^{\top},
  \qquad \widetilde U, \widetilde V \in \R^{P\times q},
\end{equation}
is the best rank-$q$ approximation of the residual image (Eckart--Young).
Note the SVD's role: it is a \emph{compression} of the target into $q$
outer products --- nothing about null spaces. Smooth, cloud-like images need
small $q$; images with sharp lines need larger $q$. (Measured: $0.6\%$ error
at $q=160$ for a cloud target; up to $9\%$ for crack-network targets at
$q=220$.)

\paragraph{Step 2: lift pixels to samples.} We need sample-level columns
$U \in \R^{D\times q}$ whose pooled version is $\widetilde U$, i.e.\
$\pool U = \widetilde U$. Since $\pool$ averages $k$ samples into one pixel,
any right inverse works; we use smooth linear interpolation between block
centers plus one exact piecewise-constant correction, so
$\pool(\mathrm{lift}(\widetilde U)) = \widetilde U$ holds \emph{exactly}
while the columns stay smooth. Same for $\widetilde V \to V$.

\paragraph{Step 3: the projection correction (fixed-point iteration).}
One wrinkle remains: the picture uses $\pool \Pperp V$, not $\pool V$, and
\begin{equation}
  \pool \Pperp V = \pool V - \pool \X\, G^{-1} \X^\top V
\end{equation}
differs from $\pool V = \widetilde V$ by the pooled shadow of a
\emph{rank-$N$} term --- small, because generic image columns are nearly
orthogonal to the $N$ songs, but not zero. We remove it by iterating
\begin{equation}
  V \;\leftarrow\; V + \mathrm{lift}\bigl(\widetilde V - \pool \Pperp V\bigr),
  \label{eq:fixpoint}
\end{equation}
which is a classical fixed-point (Richardson) iteration: writing the error
$e = \widetilde V - \pool\Pperp V$, one step maps
$e \leftarrow \pool \X G^{-1}\X^\top \mathrm{lift}(e)$, an operator of rank
$\le N$ and small norm, so the error contracts geometrically; 2--3
iterations reach sub-percent accuracy. (This is \emph{not} power iteration
--- power iteration amplifies a dominant eigenvector; here we drive a small
residual to zero, the same principle as iterative refinement in linear
solvers.)

\paragraph{Result.} With $U$ and the corrected $\Pperp V$ in
\eqref{eq:family}, $A$ still plays the playlist exactly (anything through
$\Pperp$ is inaudible), and its block-mean picture equals the chosen image
to within the SVD truncation. The aesthetic search then moves entirely into
ordinary 2D image space: generate candidate images procedurally, score them
with perceptual metrics whose weights are fitted to the owner by pairwise
comparison (Bradley--Terry), and embed the winner. The mathematics
guarantees the two roles never interfere: \emph{the music constrains $A$ on
an $N$-dimensional subspace; the image lives on the complement.}

\section*{Summary in one line}

\begin{equation*}
  A = \underbrace{\Xn G^{-1}\X^\top}_{\text{plays the playlist}}
  + \underbrace{U (\Pperp V)^\top}_{\text{draws the image}},
  \qquad
  A x_n = x_{n+1} \ \text{exactly}, \qquad
  \pool A \pool^\top \approx T .
\end{equation*}
